# Capex de construção e equipamento: tennis courts for a 24h facility in São Paulo (1–4 quadras), 2024–2026

## How much does it cost to build one tennis court in São Paulo, per court and per m², by surface type, and what are annual maintenance costs per surface?

### Takeaway
Builder price lists found online (mostly undated, seen Oct/2026) put one complete tennis court at about R$ 80–150k for saibro, R$ 120–200k for quadra rápida (acrylic over asphalt) and R$ 100–180k for grama sintética. Over the ITF total area of about 669 m², that is roughly R$ 120–300/m². Older or undated "average" quotes of R$ 35–70k usually leave out alambrado, lighting and earthworks. Clay needs daily care (about R$ 800/month reported) plus weekly top-ups of pó de telha. No Brazilian source gave a price or interval for resurfacing a hard court. For comparison, one padel court costs about R$ 110–165k from a Brazilian maker, and one beach tennis court typically costs R$ 40–80k (up to R$ 120k).

### Inferences
- **Area basis (my arithmetic):** the ITF total area is 36.57 × 18.29 = **668.9 m²** and the doubles lines area is 23.77 × 10.97 = **260.8 m²**.
- **Price per m² of total area (668.9 m²):**

  | Surface | Basis | R$/m² |
  |---|---|---|
  | Saibro | Pratika | 120–224 |
  | Saibro | Sport It | ~198 |
  | Quadra rápida | Pratika | 179–299 |
  | Grama | Pratika | 149–269 |
  | Sintético | Pratika | 135–239 |

  Over the lines area only (260.8 m²), Pratika implies R$ 307–575/m² for saibro and R$ 460–767/m² for quadra rápida.
- **Surfacing-only checks:**
  - ORSE-13930 × 668.9 m² ≈ R$ 74k for an asphalt-acrylic surface, before the stone base and earthworks.
  - Magic Gramas turf × 668.9 m² ≈ R$ 64–94k (20 mm) or R$ 74–120k (30 mm), excluding any concrete or asphalt slab.
- **Working range for one court in SP, 2024–2026, before alambrado, lighting and cover:**
  - Saibro: about R$ 80–150k
  - Quadra rápida: about R$ 120–200k
  - Grama sintética: about R$ 100–180k

  The lower R$ 35–70k figures are undated, and at least one source says explicitly that it excludes alambrado and lighting. Validate with 3 quotes, because it is unclear whether Pratika's ranges include fence or lights.
- **Alambrado for one standalone court:**
  - Perimeter is 2 × (36.57 + 18.29) = 109.7 m. At 3.35 m height that is about 368 m² of fence.
  - Cost: about R$ 45k at the 2019 SC municipal price, up to about R$ 114k at ORSE 4118.
  - Courts placed side by side share fencing: 2 courts need about 146 m of perimeter and 4 courts in a row about 219 m.
- **Net and posts:** about R$ 1.6k (two posts at about R$ 790, net extra) to R$ 3.7k installed (ORSE 13932).
- **Clay upkeep:** R$ 800/month comes to about R$ 9.6k per court per year, before any annual re-top or reform. Usage over 24 hours means more wear and grooming time.
- **Surface choice for a 24h unattended model:** clay depends on daily staff grooming, so hard courts (acrylic) or synthetic grass suit an unattended model better. This is my inference from the maintenance findings.
- **Padel vs tennis:** a padel court costs R$ 110–165k on a 20×10 m court, against R$ 80–200k for tennis on about 669 m². Roughly 3 padel courts fit in one tennis footprint before circulation space, so padel costs more per m² but yields more bookable courts per lot.
- **Beach tennis:** the cheapest court per unit, typically R$ 40–80k.

## How much does it cost to cover a tennis court with each roofing option, and what height and area are needed?

### Takeaway
Price guides put the covering material alone at R$ 280–470/m² (galvanised sheet), R$ 400–670/m² (sandwich panel), R$ 310–520/m² (PVC tensioned membrane) and R$ 650–1,080/m² (compact polycarbonate), with the large-span structure and foundations extra. A structural-steel maker quotes R$ 180–420/m² installed for steel plus roofing. A tennis cover must span about 18–20 m with no pillars and enclose at least 669 m²; one built example is 20×40 m. It needs at least 9 m of clear height over the net for recreational play, or about 12 m for tournament level. No Brazilian price for an inflatable dome was found. My planning estimate is about R$ 180–560k per covered court depending on system, to be validated by quotes.

### Inferences
- **Area to cover per court:** about 669 m² (ITF minimum envelope) to 800 m² (the 20×40 m built example).
- **Steel plus galvalume or sandwich, MLN basis:**
  - At an 18 m span, structure at R$ 130–180/m² being 48% of cost implies a total of about R$ 271–375/m². That is **about R$ 181k–300k per court** for 669–800 m².
  - MLN's overall R$ 180–420/m² band gives R$ 120k–336k. Foundations are probably extra.
- **Toldos Demais covering prices plus MLN structure (R$ 130–180/m²), for 669–800 m²:**

  | Covering | Combined R$/m² | Per court |

## What does LED lighting for one tennis court cost, how many kW does it draw, and what lux levels are recommended?

### Takeaway
- **Lux:** recreational and club play (ITF Class III) needs about 200 lux outdoors and about 300 lux indoors, with uniformity of at least 0.6–0.7 (min/avg). Brazilian makers cite 200–300 lux for recreation and about 500 lux for competition.
- **Power:** the layouts found suggest about 1.2–4.8 kW per court for recreational lighting, and one 500-lux design used 8.6 kW.
- **Cost:** fixtures are cheap (R$ 220–410 per 200–400 W floodlight, or R$ 5.2k for an 8×150 W kit). Poles, wiring, installation and the lighting design were not priced.
- **Lifetime:** no Brazilian lifetime or warranty data for sports LEDs was found.

  - Class III covers local or small-club competition, training, school sport and recreation. — [Voltimum/Philips – recreational sports lighting](https://www.voltimum.co.uk/news/philips/recreational-sports-lighting-design)

### Inferences
- **Power from photometry (my estimate):**
  - Assumptions: 668.9 m² lit, utilisation factor 0.4–0.6, maintenance factor 0.8, LED efficacy about 140 lm/W.
  - Results: 200 lux needs about 2.0–3.0 kW, **300 lux about 3.0–4.5 kW**, and 500 lux about 5.0–7.5 kW per court.
  - This fits the 4.8 kW per-pole reading of the Iluminim layout and the 8.64 kW 500-lux design. **Plan on about 3–5 kW per court for 200–300 lux over the full court area.**
- **Fixture cost at 4.8 kW from marketplace units:** 24 × R$ 219.90 ≈ R$ 5.3k, or 12 × R$ 409.90 ≈ R$ 4.9k. Professional sports floodlights with asymmetric optics and glare control will cost more (not priced). The 9 m poles, cabling, electrical panel and installation are probably the bigger share.
- **Energy for a 24h operation, per court:**
  - At 3–5 kW for 12 night hours, 365 days: about **13,100–21,900 kWh/year** if lit continuously.
  - Under an opaque roof lit 24h: about 26,300–43,800 kWh/year.
  - Switching lights per booking through the access/reservation system would cut this in proportion to occupancy.
- **Replacement interval:** with the only rating found (25,000 h, a general-purpose floodlight), lights would need replacing after about 5.7 years at 4,380 h/year (nights only), or about 2.9 years at 8,760 h/year (24h). Sports-grade luminaires may be rated higher, but no source was captured.

### Gaps
- No prices were found for 9 m lighting poles, electrical installation, a lighting design (projeto luminotécnico) or a turnkey installed tennis lighting package in BRL.
- No L70 hours or warranty terms were found for Brazilian sports LED floodlights (Novvalight, Leox, etc.). The search budget ran out before this could be checked.
- The ITF lighting guide itself was not retrieved, and secondary sources conflict on Class I values and on how uniformity is defined.

## What total area does a 2–4 court facility need, including support areas?

### Takeaway
The ITF recommends 36.57 × 18.29 m (about 669 m²) per court, with a club/recreation minimum of about 593 m². Courts alone therefore take about 1,340 m² for 2, 2,010 m² for 3 and 2,680 m² for 4. With a reception container, WC/vestiário and circulation, plan on roughly 1,500–1,800 m² for 2 courts and 3,000–3,500 m² for 4; Sebrae's court-rental guide cites at least 3,000 m². Support areas can be containers, rented for about R$ 680–1,090/month each (SINAPI, May/2026) or bought for about R$ 20–60k each (2026 listings). An 8-camera CCTV kit (R$ 2.0–2.4k) and the ART (R$ 285.59 per ART above R$ 15k of contract value in 2026) are small next to court costs. ### Inferences
- **For tennis-court quotes in Greater SP, start with Quadra de Tênis (SP capital), SP Quadras, Allpisos and Max Quadra / TS Pisos.** Pratika Sport (Sorocaba) is the only one with a published price table. Ask every builder for the same scope:
  - surface type and area (about 669 m² per court)
  - base and drainage
  - alambrado height (about 3.35 m)
  - lighting at about 300 lux with the lighting design included
  - lead time and warranty
- **For covers,** request quotes from at least 3 suppliers (e.g. Botelho, JBA, MLN or Toldos Demais), specifying:
  - span of at least 18–20 m with no internal pillars
  - footprint of about 20 × 40 m
  - clear height of at least 9 m at the net
  - covering type (galvalume, sandwich, PVC membrane or polycarbonate)
  - whether foundations and the ART are included
